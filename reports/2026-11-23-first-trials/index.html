Look away, look back

Do open interactive world models remember a place once it leaves the screen? I ran the same test on Matrix-Game 2.0 and 3.0 on one 16 GB GPU: pan the camera away, pan it back, and check whether the original view returns.

Measure it

Does the start view come back?

I score a return as recognised by counting SIFT keypoint matches between the start frame and the return frame that survive a RANSAC homography fit. A different place gives a handful of chance matches; the same place, even re-textured and a few degrees off, gives dozens to hundreds.

LPIPS, the usual pixel-perceptual metric, barely registers MG3's memory: 0.49 with memory on against 0.56 off at the 180° turn. The model brings back the place, not the pixels. Texture is re-synthesised and the heading lands a few degrees short, because MG3 turns about 10.4 px per frame one way and 9.7 the other. Judged by LPIPS alone, MG3's memory would look like a rounding error.
Protocol

Out and back

Hold still for 8 frames, turn right at a fixed mouse rate for T frames, turn left at the same rate for T frames. Frames at tpeak − k and tpeak + k share a heading, so their similarity across all k traces a memory curve. The return frame is the hardest case.

MG2: 4 start images × 3 seeds × T ∈ {12, 24, 48, 96, 192}, plus a matched camera-still control. MG3: 4 images × 2 seeds × T ∈ {24, 48, 96, 120} × memory on/off. At these yaw rates the start view is fully off screen after ~70 frames.

MG2 up close

Inside the window, then gone

A 24-frame round trip (left) stays inside MG2's attention window and comes back nearly intact. A 192-frame round trip (right) does not: the village square is replaced by another plausible village. In the stylized-village run, the camera wanders under an eave into near darkness. That happens in 6 of 120 long pans. Dropping those runs doesn't change the result.

What MG2 is

A 1.8B causal Wan-based video model, distilled to 3 denoising steps with Self Forcing, conditioned on keyboard and mouse. It attends to the last 6 latent frames (~1 s). Its only longer-range link to the start is a CLIP embedding of the first image. That embedding keeps the style of a place but not its layout.

On the RTX 5080 it runs at 8.5 fps (13.7 GB peak), about a third of the 25 fps reported on an H100.

Making it fit

Running both on 16 GB

  • MG2 (README says ≥ 24 GB): encode a short prefix of the image condition, since the VAE converges on black padding frames (mean latent difference 0.0085 against std 1.26). Park the VAE encoder and CLIP on the CPU, and replace flash-attn with PyTorch SDPA.
  • MG3 (tested on A/H-series): keep T5-XXL on the CPU. Use int8 for every large DiT linear except the action module. Chunk the FFN over tokens, park the VAE encoder on the CPU, and stream the last 10 of 30 blocks from pinned CPU memory. The result runs at 1.8 fps at 720p with a ~12 GB peak.
  • One bug worth knowing: int8 with per-token activation quantization on MG3's action MLP rounded the ±0.1 mouse signal to zero. The camera never turned and the first run scored a "perfect" LPIPS of 0.003. Check that actions still act after any quantization.
Next

What I'd test next

  • Loop closure with parallax. Walk out and back along a path, not just rotate. Field-of-view retrieval is harder when the camera translates.
  • Time vs space. MG3 indexes memory by pose. Does it also stop the stand-still drift MG2 shows?
  • A layout metric instead of LPIPS. Depth-map or DINOv2 patch agreement, so that re-textured but correct views count as remembered.
  • Other models. Self Forcing, Matrix-Game 4.0 (engine-grounded rendering, where geometry is explicit) and V-JEPA 2.
